What can I do? Where affected families can turn

If the youth welfare office has taken your child into protective care or may do so, every day can count. Here you will find independent organisations offering free help.

1. Independent complaints services (free and often the most important first step)

Ombuds services in child and youth welfare are now available in almost all German states. They are independent of the youth welfare office, free and confidential. They advise on disputes with the office and can accompany you to meetings. They do not replace legal representation, but know the procedures and can mediate.

  • All addresses by federal state: ombudschaft-jugendhilfe.de/ombudsstellen
  • Examples: Ombudsstelle Bayern (beratung@ombudsstelle-bayern.de) · Ombudschaft Jugendhilfe NRW (tel. 0202-29536776) · Ombudssystem Baden-Württemberg · Ombudsstelle RLP at the Citizens’ Commissioner

2. Initial legal consultation (strongly recommended when a child has just been taken into protective care)

If a child is in protective care or may be taken into care, every day can count. A family law specialist can involve the family court immediately if you wish to object. If you have a low income, you can apply for legal advice aid (Beratungshilfe) and legal aid for court costs (Verfahrenskostenhilfe) at the local court (Amtsgericht) to help cover the costs.

3. Associations and parent initiatives offering peer support and advice

  • ISUV e.V. (an association for maintenance and family law issues): contact points across Germany, peer support and lawyer contacts: hilfe@isuv.de
  • Väteraufbruch für Kinder e.V. – a nationwide association that also takes up individual cases where youth welfare office actions are considered excessive
  • Eltern für Kinder im Revier e.V. (EfKiR), Essen/Gelsenkirchen – a peer support group established in 1999 for parents separated from their children, with regular meetings
  • PFAD Bundesverband der Pflege- und Adoptivfamilien e.V. – if the child has been placed with a foster family

4. Peer support on social media

A note: We cannot reliably say which Facebook groups are currently active because this keeps changing. Useful German search terms are “Jugendamt Hilfe Austausch”, “Betroffene Inobhutnahme”, “Eltern gegen Jugendamt Willkür” and “Sorgerecht Selbsthilfe”. Important: These groups can offer emotional support, but legal tips from non-professionals are often inaccurate or counterproductive. For example, “just refuse to hand over the child” can have adverse legal consequences. Use groups for emotional support and turn to a lawyer or ombuds service for legal steps.

Further contacts

  • vetopedia.org – information on taking children into protective care
  • mein-einzelfall.de
  • Petition for greater transparency, legal protection and oversight of youth welfare office measures
  • lilahilfe.org – solidarity

The first three steps in brief

  1. Contact a family law specialist. In an urgent situation, do so today.
  2. Also call the appropriate ombuds service in your federal state.
  3. Keep a written record of conversations, official decisions and deadlines.

Are you looking for the ombuds service for your federal state, or would you like to share your story? Write to us – we will help where we can.
