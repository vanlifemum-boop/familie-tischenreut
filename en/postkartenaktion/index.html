What is this campaign about?

With this postcard campaign, we draw attention to children and families spending Christmas apart and to restrictions on their contact. We want those responsible to take notice, review decisions and listen to children and parents.

Create your own Christmas card here for politicians, supervisory authorities, children’s rights organisations or the media: choose a design, a message and a recipient, add a personal message, print the free PDF and send the card with postage. You can also enter an address of your own.

Personalise your postcard

The selected sample message appears directly in the field. You can add to it, change it or delete it. “Own text only / blank text area” starts with empty fields. Your changes are retained when switching between templates while this page remains open.

Maximum 70 words and 550 characters in the postcard message, plus 80 characters for the heading. The PDF also checks the available space and does not cut anything off. For a completely blank text area, leave both fields empty and untick the greeting.

We do not tolerate insults against other people.

This campaign stands for children’s rights and respectful treatment. Insults, degrading or discriminatory statements, threats and calls for violence have no place on our postcards—whether directed at politicians, staff of authorities and institutions, families or anyone else.

You can describe your own situation. Make your criticism factual and specific. Explain what you want to change and ask understandable questions. Do not use this campaign for personal attacks.

An automatic word check blocks certain expressions. It does not recognise every problematic formulation. Please check your own message before sending it.

The story behind the postcard campaign

How did this campaign begin?

We no longer want to be apart.

This campaign began because my son Thadeo and I want to live together again. We do not want to spend another Christmas apart. We want to share our everyday lives and our Christmas celebrations again.

Christmas is a time for coming together. But when your own child is missing, a place remains empty. For me, that place has a name. Thadeo.

My son should come home again. In my view, there are no grounds that justify his continued stay in residential care. I call for less intrusive forms of support to be seriously considered and for a concrete prospect of his return to be developed with us.

Our conversations do not stay between us.

Thadeo is taken to school and brought back again. The SOS-Kinderdorf development report dated 21 April 2026 documents daily accompaniment on the journey to and from school by educational staff.

Our telephone conversations do not stay between mother and son either. Staff listen in through the loudspeaker function. In its letter dated 6 November 2026, SOS-Kinderdorf expressly confirms:

Original quotation (German):

Auch das Mithören durch unsere Mitarbeitenden über Lautsprecherfunktion wurde explizit genannt und soll fortgesetzt werden.

Translation: Listening in by our staff through the loudspeaker function was also explicitly mentioned and is to continue.

I want to be able to speak to my son without anyone listening in. I want to hear how his day went, what is on his mind and what he wishes for. And I want him with me again.

Why is my son being treated like a prisoner?

I ask this question as his mother. We do not want to spend another Christmas apart. While other families plan time together, we are fighting for the chance to share everyday life again at all.

Thadeo’s own words must be heard.

The following statement by Thadeo about teasing by other children is documented:

Original quotation (German):

„Gut, davon abgesehen, dass ganz viele Kinder meinen Namen abändern auf „Thaddäus Tentakel“ und das mag ich echt nicht.“

Translation: “Good, apart from the fact that lots of children change my name to ‘Thaddäus Tentakel’, and I really do not like that.”

When asked what he does when this upsets him, he replies:

Original quotation (German):

„Na ja, meistens versuche ich, mich fernzuhalten. Aber manchmal explodiere ich auch richtig und sage es auch häufig den Erziehern“.

Translation: “Well, mostly I try to keep away. But sometimes I really explode, and I often tell the carers too.”

His wish for the telephone calls is:

Original quotation (German):

„Es würde besser laufen, wenn die Mama selber anrufen könnte, wie früher.“

Translation: “It would work better if Mum could call herself, like before.”

The passage also describes that he first has to go to Mr Krefft and mentions the possibility of making calls in the residential group. When asked about less frequent calls, he replies in the original German: „Nein, das ist es eigentlich nicht.“ [Translation: “No, that is not really it.”]

When my child says that something hurts or burdens him, this must be taken seriously. His wishes belong in decisions about his everyday life and our contact.

Where is the plan for his return?

On 27 April 2026, a meeting described as a care-planning meeting took place. I did not attend. The original minutes expressly state: „Am Hilfeplangespräch nahm sie nicht teil.“ [Translation: “She did not attend the care-planning meeting.”] Neither Thadeo nor I appear on the list of participants. No invitation reached me. The youth welfare office states in the minutes that it sent an invitation on 24 March 2026. I dispute having received that invitation.

View the original passages about attendance

I describe this procedure as sham care planning: my child’s future was discussed without my participation. I call for care planning that genuinely involves Thadeo and me and specifically examines his return home.

Page 4 of the original minutes states:

Original quotation (German):

Für die weitere Hilfeplanung wird daher empfohlen, Telefon- und Umgangskontakte kindzentriert zu gestalten und Thadeo weiterhin in seinem Ankommen in der Einrichtung zu stärken.

Translation: For further care planning, it is therefore recommended that telephone and in-person contact be arranged in a child-centred way and that Thadeo continue to be supported in settling into the institution.

The next sentence recommends maintaining and supporting contact with the mother. The minutes do not contain a concrete reunification plan with steps and a timeframe.

Why should Thadeo settle into the residential home—and where is the concrete plan for his way home?

The sequence of dates also raises questions: the meeting took place on 27 April 2026. The minutes are dated 5 June 2026. Helen Küspert’s covering letter is dated only 10 August 2026—105 days after the meeting.

Ms Küspert, why were these minutes sent to me only months later? How was I supposed to comment in time and participate in care planning?

What is a care plan for?

A care plan should clarify the need for support, the support itself and the necessary services together with those holding personal custody and the child. Where support is provided outside the family, its longer-term perspective must also be clarified and documented. This includes examining whether and how a return to the family of origin can be achieved.

According to my account, there is as yet no court order regulating contact. I criticise the youth welfare office and the supplementary guardian, Ms Schüller, for not enabling reliable, extended contact, and Mr Kreft at SOS-Kinderdorf for refusing further visits. I call for understandable decisions, reliable contact and my child’s involvement.

Stop donating to SOS Children’s Villages.

My allegation is that children’s rights are being restricted at SOS-Kinderdorf in my son’s case. Support our postcard campaign with stamps and draw attention to the situation of children in residential care.

My child is not the only child spending Christmas far from his family. This campaign is also intended to make other affected families visible and draw attention to shortcomings in SOS Children’s Villages and other residential homes. It is addressed to all children living in Germany. Their origins do not change their rights.

Please take part. Write a postcard. Ask questions. Give children and their families a voice. Every card carries our questions to the places where decisions are made.
